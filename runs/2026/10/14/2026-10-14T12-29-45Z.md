## Problem and existing work

The Erdős–Straus conjecture asks for positive integers `x,y,z` with `4/n = 1/x + 1/y + 1/z` for every `n ≥ 2`. It suffices to establish it for primes `p ≡ 1 (mod 24)`. The conjecture remains open in the cited 2026 literature.

Prior art explicitly credited:

- Erdős Problems, #242: https://www.erdosproblems.com/242
- Kyle Bradford, *Elementary Patterns from the Erdős–Straus Conjecture*, Integers **25** (2025), https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/z54/z54.pdf — divisor-square criteria; the two-target reduction below is a reformulation, **not a new theorem**.
- Xinyang Jiang, *Counting Type I and Type II solutions of the Erdos-Straus equation*, Sept 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09204 — related exact residue counts and local obstructions.
- Xiaoping Xu, *Congruence Classes ... II: Wild Solutions*, Sept 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29250 — recent parametric progress, explicitly leaves the general conjecture open.
- Mihnea and Dumitru, finite verification through `10^18` (reported), https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00128 — our finite scan **does not improve the known verification bound**.

## Exact algebraic reduction

Let `p ≡ 1 (mod 4)` be prime and choose `a=3+4t`, `0 < a < p`, `x=(p+a)/4`, `M=px`. Then `gcd(a,M)=1`, since `a=4x-p` and `x<p`. Subtraction gives

```
4/p - 1/x = a/(p*x).
```

The remaining two unit fractions exist iff there is a positive divisor `D | M^2` with

```
D ≡ -M (mod a).
```

Indeed, `(a*y-M)(a*z-M)=M^2`, and because `M` is invertible mod `a`, the complementary factor `E=M^2/D` automatically obeys `E ≡ -M (mod a)`; thus `y=(M+D)/a`, `z=(M+E)/a` are positive integers.

Since `p∤x`, a divisor `D|p²x²` has `p`-valuation 0, 1, or 2. The 0 and 2 cases are complementary. Using `p ≡ 4x (mod a)`, the exact criterion is

```
There exists h | x² such that
 (Type II)  h ≡ -x (mod a), OR
 (Type I)   4h ≡ -1 (mod a).
```

This is the known Bradford-style two-target condition in a symmetric, residue-state form. **It is equivalent to existence at this x, not a guarantee for every prime.**

## Divisor-pair route (additive combinatorics)

The Type-II condition has an especially transparent equivalent:

> There exist positive divisors `r,s | x` with `a | (r+s)`.

**Sufficiency**: put `k=(r+s)/a`. Then `y=p*(x/r)*k` and `z=p*(x/s)*k` are positive integers, and

```
1/y + 1/z = (r+s)/(p*x*k) = a/(p*x).
```

**Necessity for Type II**: write `h|x²` uniquely via its prime-power exponents as `h=x*r/s`, taking for every prime `q^e || x` the excess of the exponent of `q` in `h` above `e` into `r`, and the deficiency below `e` into `s`. Then `r,s|x`, `gcd(r,s)=1`, and, since `x/s` is coprime to `a`, `h ≡ -x (mod a)` iff `r ≡ -s (mod a)`.

This suggests attacking the conjecture by studying **divisor sumsets modulo variable `a`**, rather than directly finding three denominators.

### Provable sufficient pigeonhole criterion

Let `S` be the distinct unit residue classes modulo `a` represented by the positive divisors of `x`. If

```
|S| > phi(a)/2,
```

then there must be two residues of `S` opposite to one another modulo `a`, because the unit classes for odd `a` split into exactly `phi(a)/2` disjoint pairs `{u,-u}`. Hence Type II exists by the divisor-pair construction. This is elementary and unconditional **where its hypothesis holds**, but the hypothesis is not yet proved to hold for all remaining primes.

## Reproducible modular-state algorithm

Instead of enumerating every divisor of `x²`, factor `x`, and maintain the attainable residue set `R` modulo `a`. For a prime power `q^e || x`, update

```
R_next = union_{j=0}^{2e} q^j * R (mod a).
```

Store one concrete divisor for each residue to reconstruct a solution. Each update has at most `a` states, avoiding a potentially large Cartesian enumeration of integer divisors. Test residues `-x` and `-4^{-1}` modulo `a`, and verify every output by the exact equality

```
4*x*y*z == p*(x*y+x*z+y*z).
```

## What failed / what is missing

1. A large finite verification, including the same extremal case as the prior run, **does not imply** existence for arbitrary prime `p`.
2. A proof that `|S|>phi(a)/2` always holds for some *small* `a` is too strong for the tested window: the 14 exceptions show it fails for `a=3,7,...,143`. A more careful sumset or varying-`a` statement is needed.
3. Divisor-square residue criteria are already known; re-deriving them is not a scientific breakthrough or new proof.
4. No pointwise arithmetic theorem has been established that forces either target residue for **every** hard prime.
